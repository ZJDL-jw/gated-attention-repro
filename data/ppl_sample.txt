The transformer architecture has become the dominant approach for sequence modeling across natural language processing, computer vision, and speech. At its core lies the scaled dot-product attention mechanism, which allows every token to attend to every other token in the sequence. While powerful, this mechanism has a curious and well-documented behavior: a disproportionate amount of attention mass is often placed on the very first token of the sequence, regardless of its semantic relevance. This phenomenon, known as the attention sink, can degrade a model's ability to use long-range context effectively.

Recent work shows that inserting a simple, query-dependent gating operation immediately after the attention output can substantially mitigate this issue. The gate, implemented as a sigmoid applied to a small set of learned parameters derived from the query projection, introduces both non-linearity and input-dependent sparsity into the otherwise purely linear value-to-output mapping. As a result, the model learns attention patterns that distribute mass more evenly across the sequence, improving training stability and long-context extrapolation.

Understanding why such a small change yields such consistent gains is an active area of research. The dominant hypothesis is that the gate breaks the low-rank bottleneck between the value projection and the output projection, while simultaneously acting as a dynamic selector that filters irrelevant context on a per-token basis.
